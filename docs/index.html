A little perspective makes all the difference.

A nearby object moves farther between the two sensor views than a distant one. This shift, called disparity, is what the driver uses to estimate depth.

Find the same point twice.

Move the distance slider. The subject shifts horizontally in the right view: the closer it is, the larger the disparity. Stereo matching searches for these correspondences across the images.

Depth is an estimate.

Correspondences form a lower-resolution disparity field. Textureless walls, reflections and hidden edges can produce unreliable matches. The pipeline rejects and filters these before using the result.

Blur follows distance.

The subject remains in focus. Background blur depends on its separation from the focus plane. Edge-aware filtering and compositing help avoid spilling that blur over the subject.

This is a simplified interactive diagram, not an actual camera recording or a simulation of the full matching algorithm.

1

Align the views

Stereo calibration corrects vertical offset and roll before matching.

2

Match and verify

Census descriptors and four-path SGM find disparity. Confidence and left/right checks reject unreliable matches.

3

Stabilize depth

Hole filling, temporal smoothing and guided filtering refine depth while preserving image edges.

4

Composite the picture

Subject analysis sets focus and exposure; the GPU combines the sharp subject with a depth-dependent blur.

Why the second view is smaller

The main image stays at 1920×1080. The second sensor supplies 960×540 inside a packed 2448×1088 stream. Matching works at approximately 640×360, then guided filtering aligns the depth estimate to the output. Two full 1080p60 streams would exceed the camera’s available USB bandwidth. This layout leaves the main picture at full resolution while using the second view for geometry.

Depth gets less reliable closer than roughly 0.5 m. Transparent surfaces, repetitive patterns and occlusions can still cause artifacts; this is not a precision depth sensor.

What you get

The PlayStation 5 HD Camera has two Full HD sensors. This driver uses the main image for your video and the second view to estimate scene depth.

Native 1080p at 60 fps

Every program gets Full HD at 60 frames per second from the camera's own sensor: no upscaling. Sony's firmware stops at 30 fps in 1080p; the driver's firmware change allows 60.

Background blur from real depth

Depth comes from the two sensors (stereo matching on the GPU), so the blur follows your actual outline and distance. Switch it like Windows' own camera effects: Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Cameras → PS5 Camera.

Focus on your silhouette

Ears, hair and headphones are traced along your silhouette, to help keep them in focus while blurring the room behind you. Difficult edges can still produce artifacts.

Exposure on you

With bokeh on, brightness is metered on your head, not the whole frame: sitting in front of a bright window no longer leaves you as a dark shape.

Clean picture in dim rooms

Noise reduction follows motion to reduce trails. Automatic anti-flicker helps reduce the stripes and pulsing that mains-powered lamps cause.

Works in every program

On Windows the camera shows up as “PS5 Camera” in Zoom, Teams, Discord, Telegram, OBS, browsers and the Camera app, with no kernel driver and no test mode.

How it works on Windows

Everything runs in user mode with Windows' own camera stack.

Plug in

The camera starts in boot mode; the driver's service uploads its firmware through Windows' built-in WinUSB.

The camera appears

It re-connects as a standard UVC camera, served by the regular Windows driver.

Depth on the GPU

A Device MFT inside Windows Camera Frame Server computes depth (census + SGM) and the blur with Direct3D 11.

Any app uses it

Programs see “PS5 Camera” through Media Foundation, WinRT and DirectShow.

Install

The camera needs a USB 3 port: on USB 2.0 it only delivers 640×400. No adapter is needed.

Windows 11

  • Download PS5CameraDriver.msi (in Russian: -ru.msi) and run it.
  • Choose “PS5 Camera” in your programs. Bokeh is on after the first installation.
  • Needs administrator rights. Until releases are code-signed, SmartScreen may warn about an unknown publisher.
  • Unattended, from an administrator prompt:
msiexec /i PS5CameraDriver.msi /qn BOKEH=on

Linux

  • From PS5CameraDriver-linux.zip; systemd and udev required. Choose “USB Camera-OV580” (plain camera) or, with bokeh, “PS5 Camera”.
  • --bokeh on adds the experimental blur: x86_64 with glibc 2.34+ (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 35 or newer), a GPU with Vulkan 1.1. The installer installs the v4l2loopback kernel module; with Secure Boot, choose “Enroll MOK” on the next reboot.
sudo bash install.sh --bokeh on

macOS

  • From PS5CameraDriver-macos.zip; the camera at 1080p, without bokeh. Choose “USB Camera-OV580” in your program.
  • Needs Command Line Tools: xcode-select --install.
sudo bash install.sh

A winget package (KROU4.PS5CameraDriver) is under review. The installer downloads Sony's original camera firmware from third-party copies on GitHub, checks it and applies the driver's changes. On Windows an installation without internet access still completes and the service fetches the firmware later; on Linux and macOS the installer needs internet access, or put the original next to it as sony-firmware.bin. Questions

Answers to what people ask about the PS5 camera on a PC.

Can I use the PS5 HD Camera on a PC?

Yes. This free, open-source driver makes the PlayStation 5 HD Camera (CFI-ZEY1) a regular webcam: on Windows 11 at native 1920×1080 and 60 fps with optional background blur from real depth, on Linux at 1080p (bokeh experimental) and on macOS at 1080p without bokeh.

Do I need an adapter for the PS5 camera on PC?

No adapter is needed: plug the camera into a USB 3 port. On a USB 2.0 port the camera only delivers 640×400.

Does the PS5 camera work in Zoom, Teams, Discord and OBS?

Yes, in every program that uses webcams: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram, OBS Studio, web browsers and the Windows Camera app. On Windows, and on Linux with bokeh, the camera is called “PS5 Camera”; on macOS and on Linux without bokeh, “USB Camera-OV580”.

How does the background blur work without AI?

The camera has two image sensors. The driver compares the two images with a stereo algorithm (census transform and semi-global matching) on the graphics card, which estimates a lower-resolution disparity field aligned to the full-resolution image, and blurs what lies behind the person like a camera lens with a shallow depth of field. No neural networks are used.

What computer do I need?

On Windows: Windows 11, a USB 3 port and a graphics card with Direct3D 11; integrated graphics count. Processing runs on the GPU; speed depends on the graphics card, scene and settings. Current release measurements are in the performance section. On Linux the bokeh needs Vulkan 1.1; the plain camera on Linux and macOS needs no GPU.

Is it safe? Does it install a kernel driver?

On Windows there is no kernel code and no test mode. The effect runs in user mode as a Device MFT inside Windows Camera Frame Server, and the camera's boot mode uses Windows' built-in WinUSB driver: the installer adds a certificate created on your computer to the trusted stores to install that driver package and deletes its private key right away, so nothing else can be signed with it. On Windows nothing is sent over the network except the firmware download. On Linux the bokeh uses the v4l2loopback kernel module, which the installer installs. The source code is public under GPL-3.0.

Why does the installer download Sony firmware?

The camera needs firmware every time it is plugged in. Sony's firmware is not redistributed: the installer downloads the original image from third-party copies on GitHub, checks its SHA-256 and applies the driver's 90-byte change (1080p at 60 fps, the depth mode, automatic exposure). On Windows an installation without internet access still completes and the service fetches the firmware later; on Linux and macOS the installer needs internet access or the original placed next to it as sony-firmware.bin.

Does it work with the PS4 camera?

No. The driver is for the PlayStation 5 HD Camera (CFI-ZEY1) only. The PS4 camera is a different device and is not supported, even through a USB adapter.

What are the limitations?

One program uses the camera at a time, as with any webcam. The camera measures depth from about half a metre: anything closer is blurred unevenly. Bokeh on Linux is experimental, and macOS has no bokeh (it would need a camera system extension, which macOS only runs with an Apple developer signature).

Is it free?

Yes. The driver is free and open source under the GNU GPL 3.0. Sony's camera firmware is not part of the project.
